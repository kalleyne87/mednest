Case study

A clinical library that admits what it does not know

MedNest Internal Medicine is a fictional practice. Its staff ask questions in plain language and get cited answers from the practice's own documents.

The problem

Medical assistants and nurses constantly look things up: danger signs, testing rules, patient advice. Answers live in long PDFs, and a wrong or half-remembered one costs time at best and safety at worst.

What the staff member sees

Cited answersEvery answer lists the documents and sections it came from, with the passage one click away.
Honest gapsIf the library does not cover a question, the tool says so and flags it instead of guessing.
Follow-upsThe tool remembers the last few turns, so a short follow-up question works.

What happens behind it

  1. The page sends the question and recent history to the SpecialistAI chat API on Azure (hybrid search, semantic ranking, guardrails).
  2. Zapier logs every question and feedback tap to a Google Sheet.
  3. When a question goes unanswered, is marked not helpful, or is sent on by the user, Zapier alerts a clinical lead in Slack.
  4. A clinical lead drops a new PDF into a Drive folder. Zapier calls a small Azure Function that places it in the ingestion container, and the existing pipeline indexes it.

Why it works

The answering engine is the same one measured at 87.5% Recall@3, 92.5% Recall@5 and 97.5% faithfulness on a test set. The automation layer turns misses into a to-do list for the people who own the content, so the library improves with use.

Limits to know about

This is a demo. Login is for show, the library holds public documents only, and the tool is reference material that does not replace clinical judgment.
